Page structure

Every top-level page (Photos, Projects, Blogging, Art, About, Portfolio…) follows the same skeleton: a fixed .navigation bar, a .title-container holding the "SEE:" sub-nav + the page's <h1 class="page-title"> + an <hr> underline, and then the page's actual content wrapped in .content (or .container, its older sibling). 

<div class="title-container">
  <div class="nav-links">...SEE: writing / photography / art...</div>
  <h1 class="page-title">Page Name</h1>
  <hr>
</div>

<div class="content">
  ...page-specific content...
</div>

Bounds: why content has to stay a .content

.title-container and .content (defined once, shared, in styles/stylesheet.css) both resolve to width: 80%; margin: 0 auto;, and both get capped at max-width: 1200px on screens ≥1500px wide. Reusing the literal class name .content — rather than inventing a page-specific wrapper — is what keeps a page's content flush with its title's left/right edges at every viewport width, including that large-screen cap. A bespoke class name (e.g. an old .photos-wrap) quietly misses the cap and drifts wider than the title above it on big monitors.

Do / don't

Do <div class="content">...</div>

Add page-specific layout (flex/grid direction, gap, inner padding) to a page's own .content rule — but never redeclare its width or margin-left/margin-right.
Don't <div class="my-page-wrap">...</div>

A one-off class name isn't covered by the shared bounds or the ≥1500px cap — it will silently overflow past the title on large screens.
